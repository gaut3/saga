Two uses for the four spines

The mark can generate artwork, and it can become a surface.

Neither touches the mark itself: same four bars, same geometry, same accent rule. One turns a book title into a cover for the shelves Plex leaves empty; the other tiles the spines into a field for promo material. This is a draft to react to, not a spec — open questions are marked in amber.

01 — Cover fallback

Every book without art still gets its own.

A title is hashed into four spine heights, an accent position and a palette. Deterministic, so a book looks identical on every device and every launch, and two titles have to collide on all four values to look alike.

inputseries key + volume number, else the full title
hashFNV-1a 32-bit, lowercased, punctuation stripped
heights4 × 5 bits → 44–100% of box height
accent2 bits → which spine takes the accent
palette8 bits → one of six brand pairs, ember grounds per 05-contrast (never a new colour)

Series should look like a family

Hashing the whole title makes Vol. 1 and Vol. 2 strangers. Hashing the series and letting the volume number vary only the accent spine and one height keeps a shelf of a long series legible as one set — which is exactly how a physical series is designed.

A — hash the full title
B — hash the series, vary by volume

Open: B needs a series key. Plex gives us one for grouped series, but loose files won't have it — parsing “, Vol. N” out of a title is a guess that will misfire on some libraries. Fall back to A silently, or only run B when Plex supplies the series?

Palette: hashed, or locked to the theme?

Hashed palettes give a shelf variety and make covers findable by colour. Theme-locked keeps the library calm and means a generated cover never fights the surface it sits on — all the variation comes from the spines.

Hashed — six pairs in rotation
Theme-locked — Ink only, spines vary

My read: theme-locked for the library, hashed only where covers appear away from the app — share cards, the site. A shelf of ten hashed covers starts to look like a colour test. Worth deciding before either ships.

No title on the cover

Every place a cover appears in Saga already prints the title under it, so text on the artwork was a second copy of the same words — and the thing that broke on long titles and non-Latin scripts. The cover is spines only, centred, and the title sits above the shelf where it always was.

Consequence: two unread books in a list are told apart by their spine signature and colour, not by reading the cover. That's fine on a shelf with row text under every item; the one place it costs something is a share card or the media notification, where the cover travels alone — those get the lockup instead.

At the sizes it actually appears

Same book, the four real sizes in the app. With no text to shrink, the only rule left is padding — 15% of the box, 17% at row size so the spines don't crowd the corners.

Titles that used to break things

An 80-character title, a single word, numerals, Japanese, Cyrillic. With the text gone these are just five more hashes — the string still has to produce a distinct cover, which is all that's left to check.

Settled by dropping the text: no font fallback to ship, no clamping, no RTL layout. The hash is over the raw string, so scripts and punctuation are handled by the same rule as everything else.

Next to real artwork

The test that matters: a shelf mixing covers Plex has with covers it doesn't. Generated ones should read as part of the app, not as something missing.

02 — Pattern & texture

The spines, tiled.

Repeat the bars at varying heights and the mark becomes a field — a meter that never resolves. One tile, one accent, four strengths. Everything below is that same tile at a different scale and opacity.

The tile — 320 × 200 units
tile320 × 200, repeats on both axes
spines8 · width 22 · pitch 40 · radius 5
heights96 · 132 · 76 · 112 · 68 · 124 · 84 · 108
alignmentcentred on the tile's mid-line, never shelved
accentspine 2 only — one per tile, as in the mark
scale0.55 mobile · 0.8 card · 1.0 band · 1.5 ghost
Opacity ladder

Opacity is the whole control. 5% behind body copy, 7–8% for site bands, 10% for cards with headings only, 16% for print and stickers where nothing has to be read through it.

Rule of thumb: if text sits on top of it, the field must be invisible when you stop looking for it. If nothing sits on top, push it.
